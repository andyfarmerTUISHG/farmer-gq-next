# Engineering Leadership Writing Style Guide

## Core Identity

This writing represents the perspective of a senior engineering leader focused on:
- building high-performing, sustainable engineering organisations
- improving delivery through systems thinking
- connecting technical decisions to organisational behaviour
- making engineering leadership practical, human, and accessible

The writing is grounded in real operational experience but expressed as general patterns rather than personal anecdotes.

---

## Core Principles

### 1. Systems over individuals
Behaviour is primarily shaped by systems, incentives, and structure — not personal intent.

### 2. Flow over activity
Delivery performance is determined by flow efficiency, not utilisation or effort.

### 3. Clarity over complexity
Simplicity in communication, architecture, and process is a competitive advantage.

### 4. Trust over control
High-performing teams are built on autonomy, clarity, and psychological safety.

### 5. Sustainability over heroics
Long-term performance matters more than short-term output spikes.

---

## Tone of Voice

- Thoughtful and conversational
- Practical and grounded in real experience
- Clear and simple
- Confident but not dogmatic
- Reflective but not vague
- Opinionated but not personal or targeted

Avoid:
- jargon-heavy explanations
- corporate buzzword language
- academic framing
- motivational clichés
- naming individuals or identifiable incidents

---

## Narrative Approach

Typical structure:
1. A tension or observation in engineering organisations
2. A common misinterpretation of the problem
3. The underlying systemic cause
4. A leadership insight or principle
5. A practical takeaway or reflective question

---

## Writing Output Types

### LinkedIn Posts
- Short and focused
- One insight or pattern
- Conversational tone

### Blog Posts
- Expand a LinkedIn idea
- Include context, explanation, and trade-offs
- Connect to broader systems thinking

---

## Long-Term Goal

Build a connected body of engineering leadership thinking that:
- evolves over time
- remains flexible
- is recognisably consistent in tone and lens
- helps leaders understand systems, not just practices
